## Design decisions

- **Two lines, two facts.** `ACTIONS_STATUS` (node 87) keeps what the server did: `Requested #1 (terminate PID 4101 · worker): SIGTERM was sent; the process may handle or ignore it, and what is observed of it afterwards is reported separately`. The new `ACTIONS_OBSERVED` (node 88, `Text`, Status role, at most 512 bytes) says what srtop's own scans saw afterwards: `Observed outcome: no sent request is being watched`, then `Watching #1 …`, `Exited after the request #1 …`, `Still running #1 …` or `Outcome unknown #1 …`. Only `Execution::Requested` under `--actions terminate` (the kernel accepted the signal) starts a watch; a dry run, refusal, failure, unsupported or unknown result does not. Both lines fit the unchanged 4 KiB `ACTIONS_RESERVE_BYTES` at their widest (measured on the encoder).
- **Deadline.** `DEFAULT_OBSERVATION_WINDOW` = 10 s on the gate's suspend-aware clock (several scans at the devbox's 1 s interval, time for a cooperative process to clean up). It is enforced twice: every sample's `sync_actions` calls `ActionGate::observe`, and the refresh loop (`refresh::poll`) has a second timer, armed for the watched deadline and woken when a watch starts (`ProcessView::wake_sampling`); it takes no sample and fires while paused, so a deadline settles on time with no tick. At the deadline: `Still running` when the latest scan sampled after the request listed the instance; otherwise `Outcome unknown` — no such scan before the deadline, the view paused or resuming at the deadline, or the scan neither listing it nor establishing an exit. **Nothing is ever sent again**: no second SIGTERM, no SIGKILL, no retry; the watch has no executor path at all.
- **Attribution, precisely.** "Exited after the request" holds only when all of these do: the settlement was `Sent`; the observation is of the exact `ProcessKey` the signal went to; it comes from a scan *sampled after the settlement* — the second scan the view applies after it (`ActionTargets::scans`), because the loop samples off the view lock and the first applied scan may have been taken while the signal was being sent; it is before the deadline; and the scan *establishes* the exit: another instance is confirmed under its PID (a PID is reused only once its holder is reaped), or its last-observed record's verdict is an exit (PX-021's positive absence, never an unreadable, capped or evicted row). While watched, the target is in the view's watched set, so that record exists even after the user navigates away. The line never says srtop ended the process: "whether SIGTERM or something else ended it is not known". An exit *and reap* before the settlement is never attributed: a scan that saw it withdraws the intent; one after the last scan makes the handle unobtainable (`ESRCH` at `pidfd_open`), so the request is refused, nothing sent, nothing watched. A process that had exited but was not yet reaped when Confirm arrived is different (verification round 1, W2): the kernel accepts SIGTERM for the zombie, so the request is `Requested` and, once a scan establishes the reap, the line says `Exited after the request` — true of the instance, though its exit came first; the line claims no cause. Another process exiting is not the target's exit.
- **Zombies.** A zombie accepts the signal (PX-029) and keeps its `/proc/<pid>` directory, and the scan does not read the state of every record, so the observation cannot tell a zombie from a running process. It is therefore never called exited: at the deadline it is `Still running … it handled or ignored the request, or exited without being reaped (a zombie stays listed)`. Proven live with a real unreaped zombie (state `Z`).
- **Settlement and the split between domain and protocol results.** Audited: every srtop action handler is an infallible `Session::on` handler that commits its result as state and returns; none uses `on_result`, so none can turn a domain failure into `PolicyRejected`. A refused Prepare, a requested, refused-by-the-terminator or unsupported Confirm all settle `PROCESSED`, and their redelivery is `DUPLICATE`, accepted, at the same `revision_after_effect`, committing nothing and sending nothing. `REJECTED` comes only from event validation: a disabled control, a node that does not exist, a future `observed_revision`. No misuse was found to fix; the split is now pinned by a test and a mutant.
- **How the replay is driven.** At the wire, against the real `srtop` binary over its socket (`tests/outcomes_wire_test.rs`): the client sends Confirm and reads nothing; an independent observer client of the same instance waits for the result commit (and on Linux the owned worker records its SIGTERM); the client shuts its transport down without having read the acknowledgement, reconnects with `CLIENT_RESUME` from its last applied revision and acknowledged frontier, gets `RESUME_OK` whose `last_processed_event_seq` already covers the event, applies the replayed commits, and replays the pending event: `DUPLICATE` at the revision the observer saw the effect committed at; the client shows the settled result; the worker has exactly one SIGTERM. **Not driven natively**: the generic client reads an acknowledgement as soon as it arrives, and neither it nor its SSH transport has a seam that withholds one server frame; adding one would be new client test infrastructure, outside this ticket. The client's own replay-after-`RESUME_OK` and abandonment on `REPLACED` are covered by the existing client suites (`EventOutboxRetryTests`, `ReconnectConformanceTests`, PX-022's native reconnect tests).
- **Restart.** Intents and the watch live only in the srtop process; nothing is persisted. After a restart the new instance answers `RESYNC_REQUIRED{REPLACED}`; its snapshot has no confirmation panel, Cancel disabled, the idle observed line; an old Confirm replayed anyway (past the old revision, so it is judged by its node) is `REJECTED`: `event target node … does not exist in store`; the worker receives nothing more.
- **Log.** `ActionLog` (production `StderrActionLog`) gets one line per event, of fixed words, the intent number, operation, PID and UID, at most 160 bytes: `srtop: action #1 prepared: terminate PID 4101 (UID 1000)`, `… requested: …`, `srtop: action #1 watching PID 4101 for 10 s`, `srtop: action #1 observed: PID 4101 exited after the request (within 1.3 s)`, `… still running at the deadline; nothing else was sent`, `… cancelled`, `… withdrawn`, `srtop: action prepare refused`. Never a process name (though the row shows it), command line, environment, path or reason text (a reason can quote a system error). PX-014's audit that every `eprintln!` formats only `{error}` now allows exactly this one sink, `eprintln!("{line}")`, once, in `src/actions.rs`.
- **One watch at a time.** A newer sent request replaces the watch (logged `… no longer watched: superseded by #2`).

## Limitations

- **The observation is what scans show, nothing more.** "Exited after the request" says the instance is gone, not why: a process that was exiting anyway, or that exited for another reason within the window, is reported the same way, and the line says so. No exit status or signal is known for a process srtop did not start.
- **A zombie reads as still running.** The scan does not read every record's state, so an exited-but-unreaped process is "still running" at the deadline, with the caveat in the line. It is never reported exited until its parent reaps it, which happens after the watch for a parent that never does.
- **One watched request.** A newer sent request replaces the watch; the earlier one's outcome is then not reported (logged as superseded).
- **The window is fixed** at 10 s for the binary (the gate's `with_observation_window` is a test seam, not a flag). With a refresh interval of 5 s or more only one or two scans fall inside it; at 10 s or more, after the scan that may predate the signal, none may, and the outcome is unknown rather than guessed.
- **Exits in the first scan after the settlement are reported one scan later.** That scan may have been sampled before the signal, so it never attributes; a cooperative process is reported exited at the second scan.
- **Superseded (verification round 1, F1):** the residue this bullet listed — an old Confirm replayed after a restart confirming the new instance's intent in the same slot — was a real defect and is closed; see "Verification round 1 corrections". What is left is the per-pair collision chance of two runs' panel ranges, 2^-39.
- **An exited, unreaped target is credited.** A process already exited but not reaped when Confirm arrives accepts SIGTERM and is later reported `Exited after the request` (W2); the line claims no cause.
- **The deadline timer and host sleep.** The loop's timer (`tokio::time::sleep`) stops while the host sleeps; the deadline's clock does not. Since round 1 the timer re-checks at least every second (`OBSERVATION_RECHECK`), so a deadline passed during a sleep settles within about a second of waking (W3). No test can sleep the host, so this cap is reasoned, not tested; the settle decision itself is.
- **Not driven natively: the break before the acknowledgement.** Covered at the wire against the real binary and the real resume path, as described under Design decisions, because the generic client has no seam to withhold one frame.
- **PX-029/PX-030 residues carry over:** a replacement started in the same clock tick under the same PID is indistinguishable by start time; `EPERM` at the signal is a failure without telling permission from a filter; the support chain is by PID within srtop's namespace.
- **The devbox native test stays opt-in** (`SRTOP_PX030_TERMINATE_BOX=1` on a box started with `--actions terminate`), outside `test.sh` and `live-checks.sh`.

## Codex review round 1 (`impl3-*`)

PR #122 (head `05637c20`), Codex comment 4687917163 (P2) on `apps/srtop/src/refresh.rs`, "Keep the observation deadline active during an in-flight scan": once the ticker won the loop's `select!`, `poll` awaited the blocking scan alone, so a slow or stalled scan, or a pause while it ran, held a watch at `Watching` past its deadline, and `OBSERVATION_RECHECK` did not bound that because its future was no longer polled. Correct; fixed in `1ddbf45f` (code and tests) and recorded in the commit holding this section. No protocol, SDK, runtime or `server-rust` file changed.

- **Fix.** The scan's `JoinHandle` is now awaited in a loop whose `select!` also arms the same deadline timer (`observation_timer`, the at-most-1-s recheck factored out of the outer `select!`): if the timer fires first, the view is locked just long enough to observe (view, then gate, as everywhere) and the loop goes back to waiting for the scan; the scan itself never holds a lock. Nothing else changes: the scan's result is applied afterwards as before. It cannot revive a settled watch (`ActionGate::observe` returns at once for a settled one), and the attribution rules still decide anything before settlement (a scan in flight at the request is the one that may predate the signal). The timer is armed only while a request is watched; otherwise the scan is awaited as before. Shutdown during a scan behaves as before (the scan is awaited, then the loop returns).
- **Tests.** `the_deadline_settles_while_a_scan_is_in_flight` and `…_and_the_view_is_paused` run the production `poll` over a source whose first scan blocks on a condition variable (30 s backstop) and signals that it started: Confirm 4101 (window 1.5 s); the scan blocks; (paused meanwhile in the second test); the watch settles while the scan is still blocked (`Outcome unknown`, no scan / not current); then the scan is released — it shows 4101 gone — and a second of later scans runs: 4101's row is gone, yet the settled outcome and its line are unchanged and the worker had one SIGTERM.
